The Iron Bowl

Alabama vs. Auburn, every year since 1948 and 90 times since 1893. Alabama leads the series 52–37–1, including 48–30 since the rivalry resumed in 1948.

How it started

Alabama and Auburn first played on Feb. 22, 1893, in Birmingham. Auburn won that one 32–22, and Alabama's records count it as the last game of its 1892 season.

The two schools met most years until 1907, when they played to a 6–6 tie in the mud, still the only tie in the series. That game is where Alabama's "Thin Red Line" picked up a new name: the Crimson Tide. Then the schools stopped playing each other for 41 years.

The state legislature finally pushed them back together. On Dec. 4, 1948, at Legion Field in Birmingham, Alabama won 55–0. It's still the most lopsided game in the series. That was also the first year of the Iron Bowl's trophy, the Omicron Delta Kappa Cup.

Birmingham hosted the game every year from 1948 through 1988, and the city gave the rivalry its name. Auburn coach Shug Jordan is credited with it: asked in 1964 about missing out on a bowl trip, he said his team already had a bowl game every year, the Iron Bowl in Birmingham. The steel city's nickname stuck. Auburn began hosting at Jordan-Hare Stadium in 1989, and in 2000 the game came to Tuscaloosa for the first time since 1901.
